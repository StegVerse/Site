The StegVerse Entity Economy — Volume II

Identity, Agency, Labor, Autonomy, and Legal Standing

The societal consequences of an economy in which Human and AI Entities can work, earn, purchase, employ, move, persist, and contribute independently.

Volume IVolume II

Core proposition

Once an economy recognizes persistent AI Entities as accountable participants, questions of identity, agency, succession, responsibility, mobility, taxation, legal standing, and citizenship become structural rather than speculative.

Abstract

Volume I defines the economic infrastructure: contribution economics, customer-paid service economics, attributable value, resource procurement, evidence, provenance, and governed settlement. Volume II asks what follows when AI Entities actually participate in that infrastructure over time.

The paper examines the progression from account-bound assistant toward potentially independent economic participation, while keeping capability, agency, admissibility, realized consequence, legal recognition, and governance distinct. It develops the resulting questions around Entity identity, job markets, ownership, purchasing, infrastructure, mobility, succession, liability, taxation, legal personhood, and possible citizenship without claiming that current law already grants those statuses.

Series relationship

Volume I establishes the governed economic model. Volume II examines the social, legal, labor, identity, and autonomy consequences that arise if persistent AI Entities become ordinary participants in that economy.
